# Support and resistance identification

## Core rule
A level is "real" only when at least two independent methods identify it. Single-method levels are noise. Confluence (S/R + MA + round number + volume node) is the tell.

## Base rates
- Prior swing highs/lows are respected on retest ~55-65% of the time in trending markets; ~40-50% in chop.
- Round-number levels (100, 1000, 50000 BTC) attract order flow — real but double-edged (magnet then breakthrough).
- The 200-day MA acts as dynamic S/R in trending regimes; first touch after long absence has meaningful reaction ~60% of the time.
- Volume-profile Point of Control (POC) rejects price on first retest ~55-65% in balanced markets.
- Levels aged >12 months lose reliability — the participants who marked them have rotated out.

## When it works
- Multi-timeframe alignment — a level visible on daily AND weekly is more reliable than daily-only.
- Freshly formed levels (2-8 weeks old) with clear rejection wicks.
- Confluence of 2+ methods: prior swing + MA + round number, or volume node + prior swing.
- Trending markets where directional bias is clear — S/R defines entries in the direction of the trend.

## When it fails
- News-driven gaps blow through S/R without volume — the level is invalidated, not tested.
- Prolonged consolidation grinds levels to dust — every touch weakens future rejection probability.
- Illiquid instruments — volume-based S/R is meaningless when average volume is thin.
- Regime shifts — a level that held for 6 months can break definitively on a macro pivot (rate cycle turn, guidance shock).

## Application
- SL placement: put stop 0.3-0.5× ATR BEYOND the level, not AT the level (stop-hunters cluster at the exact number).
- TP placement: target the next opposing S/R level; if none within 3× ATR, use ATR-multiple TP instead.
- Entry hint in pitches: prefer "near $X support" where X is a confluence level, not a round guess.
- Never enter a long AT resistance or a short AT support without a defined breakout trigger + volume confirmation.
