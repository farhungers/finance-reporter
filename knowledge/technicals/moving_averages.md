# Moving averages: 20 / 50 / 200-day

## Core rule
Moving averages are trend filters, not trade triggers. The 200-day defines the long-term regime; the 50-day defines the intermediate trend; the 20-day defines short-term positioning. Use MA SLOPE, not just price-vs-MA, as the trend signal.

## Base rates
- Price above rising 200-day = long-only bias; historical S&P edge is ~2-3× stronger long returns vs below-200-day regime.
- Golden cross (50 crosses above 200) on major indices: forward 12-month returns positive ~65-75% historically; median gain modest, not a lay-up.
- Death cross (50 crosses below 200) is often late — average drawdown from signal to bottom is smaller than pre-signal drawdown.
- 20-day MA acts as dynamic support in strong uptrends; first touch after breakout holds ~60% of the time.
- Flat/choppy MAs (slope near zero for 10+ bars) give false signals >50% — filter them out.

## When it works
- Trending markets with clear directional slope on the 50- and 200-day.
- Index-level and megacap equity — enough liquidity to make MAs meaningful.
- As a regime filter — long trades only above rising 200-day, shorts only below falling 200-day.
- Confirmation, not signal — MA alignment adds a rubric point when S/R and catalyst are also present.

## When it fails
- Ranging/choppy markets — MAs whipsaw and produce serial false crosses.
- Immediately after gap events (earnings, macro shocks) — the MA hasn't caught up.
- Small-caps and low-liquidity crypto — MAs get pushed around by single participants.
- Very-short-window MAs (5, 8-day) — high false-signal rate; use only in intraday context.

## Application
- Regime filter: pitch bias direction should match the 200-day slope on the underlying (long if rising, short/neutral if falling).
- Trend confirmation: for a "trend intact" rubric point, require price above rising 50-day for longs.
- SL reference: 50-day MA is a defensible SL anchor for multi-week swing longs — stop 0.3× ATR below the MA.
- Golden/death cross callouts belong in weekly_prep or thesis prose, not as standalone entry signals.
