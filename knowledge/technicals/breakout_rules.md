# Breakout confirmation rules

## Core rule
A breakout without volume expansion is a fakeout in waiting. Real breakouts pair a level break with 1.5-2× the 20-day average volume AND hold on retest.

## Base rates
- Unconfirmed breakouts (no volume expansion) fail within 5 sessions ~50-60% of the time.
- Volume-confirmed breakouts (>1.5× 20-day avg volume on the break bar) have follow-through 55-65% at 5-10 session horizon.
- Successful retests: after a real breakout, price pulls back to the broken level and holds ~65% of the time; that retest is the higher-quality entry.
- Opening-range breakouts (first 30-60 min US session) have higher continuation odds than late-day breakouts, which often mean-revert overnight.
- End-of-week breakouts get faded on Monday ~40% of the time — weekend risk unwinds enthusiasm.

## When it works
- Consolidation periods of 4+ weeks resolving with a clean break of a well-defined level.
- Broader-market tailwind — individual-name breakouts fail more often in a bear tape regardless of setup quality.
- Sector rotation into the name — relative-strength confirmation adds robustness.
- Catalyst-driven breaks (earnings beat, upgrade, macro data) — the reason for the break is identifiable.

## When it fails
- Thin tape / low-liquidity sessions (holidays, summer Fridays) — volume signal is meaningless.
- Extended, already-run names — breakouts from 30%+ above 50-day MA fail more often (exhaustion).
- Choppy, no-trend-in-the-index environments — even good breakouts get sold into.
- Overnight-gap "breakouts" without intraday volume follow-through — the gap IS the move.

## Application
- Never pitch a "breakout long" without volume confirmation criteria in the thesis (e.g., "on close above $X with volume >1.5× 20-day avg").
- Prefer retest entries over break-bar entries for pitches (better R, higher hit rate).
- SL for breakout trades: 0.5-1× ATR below the broken level (once broken, it becomes support).
- Fakeout risk gets a `low_star_warning` mention when volume was borderline on the break bar (1-1.3× avg).
