# PCE playbook

## What it is
Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index, released by the Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) the **last week of each month at 08:30 ET** as part of the Personal Income and Outlays report (which also releases personal income and spending same morning).

**This is the Fed's actual 2% inflation target measure.** Chair Powell repeatedly emphasizes core PCE, not core CPI, as the target series. Yet in market attention, CPI wins because it releases 2 weeks earlier — so PCE frequently gets faded when it merely confirms what CPI already priced.

Two headline numbers:
- **Headline PCE MoM/YoY** — full basket including food and energy.
- **Core PCE MoM/YoY** — excludes food and energy. The Fed's operative target.
- **Super-core (services ex housing) PCE** — the granular measure Powell cited most 2022-2024. Not always in the wire headlines but visible in tables.

## Why PCE differs from CPI
- **Basket weights** — PCE weights healthcare higher (employer + Medicare/Medicaid spending), housing lower (~15% vs CPI's 34%). Yearly divergence usually 0.3-0.7pp with core PCE running below core CPI.
- **Substitution** — PCE assumes consumers substitute when prices rise (chained Fisher index); CPI holds basket weights fixed longer.
- **Rent** — CPI's Owners' Equivalent Rent gets heavier weight and updates slower. PCE's rent proxy is less dominant.
- **Health insurance** — CPI backs into it via retained-earnings method (lagging); PCE uses direct payments (more current).

Net effect: PCE tends to be smoother, lower, and more responsive at turning points than CPI.

## Historical reaction
- **When PCE confirms the CPI narrative from 2 weeks prior:** minimal market reaction. Volatility typically compresses. This is the modal outcome.
- **When PCE diverges hot from a cool CPI:** rates + USD reprice hawkish; SPX -0.5% to -1.0%. This has happened ~15% of prints 2022-2024 and shocks markets more because CPI framed the expectation.
- **When PCE diverges cool from a hot CPI:** rate-cut relief; SPX +0.5% to +1.0%, USD sells. Also ~15% of prints.
- **Super-core surprise:** if super-core PCE MoM comes ≥5bps above or below consensus while core is in-line, second-order rate reaction can be larger than core surprise because Powell explicitly cited super-core.

## Playbook
- **Read core PCE first, then super-core, then compare to the CPI print 2 weeks prior.** The divergence is where the trade lives.
- **Personal income/spending same morning:** hot spending + weak income (savings-rate compression) = late-cycle warning that gets ignored on impact day and matters over 4-6 weeks. Watch the savings rate.
- **Fed meeting proximity:** PCE released in the 2 weeks before FOMC has amplified impact — it's the Fed's last inflation data point before the meeting. PCE in the week AFTER FOMC has less impact.
- **Sector map same as CPI** — hot core = short-duration + banks + energy outperform; cool core = long-duration tech + REITs + gold outperform.

## Common wrongness
- **Ignoring PCE because CPI released 2 weeks earlier.** When PCE diverges, positioning is stale and moves are outsized.
- **Only looking at core, not super-core.** Powell talks about super-core; the market should too.
- **Trading personal spending in isolation.** Nominal spending includes inflation — the deflated version (real spending in the same release) is the demand signal.

## Reference dates for base-rate anchoring
- PCE May 2024 (core 0.08% MoM, well below CPI-implied 0.3%): USD -80bps, SPX +0.9%.
- PCE Jul 2023 (core 0.2% MoM confirmed CPI cool trend): muted reaction (modal outcome).
- PCE Jan 2024 (core 0.4% MoM hot vs benign CPI): 2Y +12bps, SPX -0.5%, sparked the "sticky inflation" narrative for Q1 2024.
- PCE Nov 2022 (core 0.2% MoM cool confirming CPI): sustained the SPX rally into year-end.
