# Fed speak taxonomy

## The core distinction: voting vs non-voting
The FOMC has 12 voting members at any given meeting:
- Chair + Vice Chair (permanent)
- 5 other Governors on the Board (permanent, when confirmed)
- NY Fed President (permanent voter — the trading desk sits inside NY Fed)
- 4 rotating regional Fed Presidents (Chicago + Cleveland + Boston/St. Louis/Dallas/Kansas City/Minneapolis/Atlanta/San Francisco/Richmond/Philadelphia rotate each year)

Non-voting Fed presidents (the ~7 rotating-off members in any given year) still speak publicly but rarely move markets. Vote status is the first filter for whether a speech matters.

## Speaker ranking by typical market impact
1. **Chair Powell** — largest impact by wide margin. See `powell_speeches.md` for detail.
2. **Vice Chair (Jefferson currently, Brainard before)** — meaningful when speaking on policy; less so on regulation.
3. **NY Fed President (Williams)** — permanent voter, runs the trading desk; policy remarks always move markets.
4. **Voting Governors (Waller, Bowman, Cook, Kugler, Jefferson)** — Waller and Bowman disproportionately move markets due to prior track record of previewing policy shifts.
5. **Voting regional presidents (this year's four)** — moderately move markets, more so if consensus-breaking.
6. **Non-voting regional presidents** — rarely move markets. Exception: when a non-voter contradicts recent Powell tone (signals internal division).

## Playbook — how to trade Fed speak
- **In the blackout window** (10 days before FOMC through the day after): no Fed speak. Anything scheduled that ends up "impromptu" is a major deal.
- **During the intermeeting period:** speeches cluster in weeks 2-4 after FOMC. A consistent tone across 3+ speakers = the Fed is pre-committing markets to a message. Trade the setup.
- **Cross-tone signal:** when a voting hawk turns dovish (or vice versa), that speaker's move is worth 5-10x a random Fed-speak signal. Waller's Dec 2023 "cuts coming" speech moved markets more than any statement.
- **When to fade:** non-voter speeches that align with recent Powell tone are already priced in — fade any immediate reaction.
- **When to follow:** voting-member speech that diverges from recent Powell tone typically previews the next FOMC's direction. Follow.

## Speech venue matters
- **Prepared remarks at economic conference (NABE, AEA, Peterson, Brookings):** most policy-substantive.
- **Testimony to Congress:** Q&A can produce more news than opening statement.
- **Fireside chat / Q&A only (no prepared remarks):** wide-ranging, higher hit rate for market-moving lines that fall outside typical talking points.
- **Community bank / regional business speeches:** almost never market-moving. Local economy focus.
- **Media interviews (CNBC, WSJ live, Bloomberg TV):** varies by anchor. Steve Liesman + Nick Timiraos have a track record of getting policy signal.

## Nick Timiraos rule
Nick Timiraos (WSJ) has been the reliable "Fed whisperer" 2019-present. When a Timiraos article drops the weekend before FOMC discussing a specific outcome, that outcome has ~90% hit rate at the meeting. Do not fade a Timiraos framing story pre-FOMC.

## Common wrongness
- **Over-reading nuance from staffers or non-voters.** Their opinions matter for internal debate but rarely shift the median dot.
- **Trading "dovish" or "hawkish" characterizations in real-time headlines.** Bloomberg + Reuters wire summaries often mischaracterize the tone in the first 60 seconds. Wait for the full transcript excerpt before acting.
- **Ignoring speech venue.** A speech at a community bank rarely contains policy news; the same speaker at Brookings might.
- **Under-weighting Waller and Bowman.** Their track record of previewing shifts is stronger than several rotating regional presidents combined.

## Reference dates for base-rate anchoring
- Waller Nov 2023 ("in the ballpark of cuts"): sparked 3-week bond rally, SPX +6%.
- Powell Aug 2022 Jackson Hole ("some pain"): SPX -3.4% on the day; kicked off 2-month sell-off.
- Bullard 2019 pivot signal on CNBC (Jun): 2Y fell 20bps on the interview.
- Bostic Feb 2023 dovish tone (non-voter but market listened): faded by afternoon — non-voter dismissed correctly.
- Kashkari Aug 2024 divergence from Powell tone: rate-cut odds re-priced 5bps for Sep meeting.
