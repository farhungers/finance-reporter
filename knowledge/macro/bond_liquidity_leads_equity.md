# Bond and liquidity signals — the 3-2 month lead over equity bottoms

Napier's second big insight: **fixed income and monetary conditions turn before equities.** Bonds bottom 2-3 months before stocks. Fed policy inflection precedes equity recovery by 3-6 months. Watch these markets, not equities, for the earliest bottom signal.

## Bonds bottom before stocks — the lead relationship

| Bear cycle | Bond stabilization | Equity bottom | Lead time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1921 | Corporate bonds May-June | Aug 24 | ~2-3 months |
| 1932 | Bonds recover May | Jul 8 | ~2 months |
| 1949 | Fed-supported bonds, real signal was policy shift Q1 | Jun 13 | ~3-5 months |
| 1982 | Corp bonds recovered early | Aug 1982 | months |

**Mechanism:** bond traders and institutional money sense monetary/inflation dynamics before equity investors fully adjust. Bond prices reflect discount-rate changes directly; equity multiples require the credit stabilization to filter through to risk-premium repricing.

**How to read today:** track corporate bond ETFs (LQD, HYG) and Treasury yields. If credit spreads narrow and long yields range-bound for 4-6 weeks while equities are still making new lows, the equity bottom is likely weeks away.

## The dividend-vs-bond-yield inversion

**The single sharpest bottom signal.** When S&P dividend yield rises to meet (or exceed) 10Y Treasury yield, institutional rebalancing from bonds into equities becomes mechanical.

- 1949: S&P dividend yield 4.0% vs AAA corporate bond yield 2.46% → 150+ bps equity premium. WSJ noted 20 carefully selected stocks yielded 8-12%, each earning 50%+ above dividend levels (safety + income growth).
- 1932: dividend yields exceeded corporate bond yields broadly.
- 1921: widening margin as equity prices fell.
- 1982: extreme after 15 years of stagflation.

**Signal for 2026:** the current S&P dividend yield vs 10Y spread is a high-signal metric. Crossover (equity yield > bond yield) has been rare in the past 30 years. If it appears, take it seriously as a bear-bottom signal — cross-reference the other Napier convergence indicators.

## Liquidity signals — Fed policy leads by 3-6 months

Fed easing precedes equity bottoms. But *policy stance shift* matters more than balance-sheet size.

- **1921** — Fed cut discount rate May 1921, three months before Aug 24 bottom. Fed credit outstanding then fell 69% by July 1922 as recovery took hold.
- **1932** — Fed open-market purchases April preceded July 8 bottom by ~3 months.
- **1949** — Fed eased consumer credit controls March 1949 — a stance shift, not a balance-sheet expansion. Bottom June 13. Commercial bank loans bottomed *in June* (lagging indicator).
- **1982** — Volcker's shift from ideological monetarism to pragmatic disinflation-support was the pivot. Bottom Aug 1982.

**Key discipline:** watch Fed **language and stance changes**, not balance-sheet expansion (which lags). Rate cuts, reserve reductions, guidance shifts from "tightening" to "maintenance" or "accommodation" are the leading signal.

**Bank loan growth is a LAGGING indicator.** It confirms the recovery is real, but by the time loan growth turns positive, the equity bottom is usually already established.

## Sentiment signals — the "bulls and the bear"

Contrary to myth, bottoms are NOT characterized by universal pessimism. Prominent commentators, analysts, and executives issue bullish calls before the absolute bottom. What matters is **the market's indifference to news, positive or negative.**

- **1949** — WSJ published numerous positive earnings reports, dividend increases, industrial demand data in April-May. Short interest reached 16-year high (1,644,313 shares) in May. Value investors already accumulating at single-digit PEs — but the market ignored the bullish flow until June.
- **1921** — market's failure to rally decisively on good earnings noted by technical analysts as evidence bears were losing conviction. Short covering combined with low volume signaled bears abandoning positions.

**Critical signal:** the market's *indifference to news* is more diagnostic than bullish vs bearish commentary volume. When earnings beat, dividends rise, economic data improves — and the market moves flat to slightly down — that's the bear-market-exhaustion signal.

**For 2026:** create a simple metric — on days when earnings beat OR economic data surprises to the upside, does the market rally materially? Sustained flat-response for 3-4 weeks = bottom approaching.

## The 3-signal alignment at bottoms

Napier's most useful synthesis: when all three converge, bottom is close.

1. **Price stability** — end of accelerating price declines in commodities, wholesale prices, or the equity indexes themselves. Not necessarily flat prices, just the end of accelerating losses.
2. **Yield extremes** — equity yields reaching multi-decade highs vs safe assets (bonds, T-bills). Dividend-vs-bond crossover is the sharp version.
3. **Monetary policy reversal** — Fed easing signals (rate cuts, reserve moves, stance-shift language) accompanied by early credit-expansion signs.

Historical bottoms: all three aligned within a 2-8 week window before the price low.

## Actionable 2026 dashboard

For weekly synthesis when the S&P is in drawdown >15%:

**Monetary signals**
- Fed Funds rate direction (rising vs peaked vs cutting)
- Fed guidance language shift ("tightening" → "maintenance" → "accommodation")
- Reserve requirement / bank-liquidity actions
- **Signal weight: highest.** 3-6 month lead.

**Bond market signals**
- 10Y Treasury yield stability (range-bound for 4-6 weeks)
- Corporate bond spreads (IG OAS, HY OAS) narrowing or flat
- Corporate bond prices stabilizing after decline phase
- **Signal weight: high.** 2-3 month lead.

**Yield spread signals**
- S&P dividend yield vs 10Y Treasury (crossover = strong signal)
- Yield on quality dividend payers vs high-grade corporate bonds
- **Signal weight: high when at extreme.**

**Sentiment / positioning**
- Short interest at multi-year highs — AND declining for 2-3 reporting periods
- Put/call ratio elevated but declining
- Mutual-fund cash levels elevated
- IPO drought
- Market indifference to positive earnings surprises
- **Signal weight: moderate.** Confirms convergence but not sufficient alone.

**Commodity / inflation signals**
- CRB/oil/copper stability (no new lows for 6+ weeks)
- PPI / CPI momentum breaking (deceleration in the harmful direction — deflation ending in a deflationary bear, or inflation breaking in a stagflationary bear)
- **Signal weight: moderate.** Regime-context dependent.

## Common wrongness

- **Waiting for confirmation from equity price action alone.** Bonds and liquidity have already turned by then.
- **Treating balance-sheet size as the Fed signal.** Language and stance change matter more; balance-sheet moves lag.
- **Treating single crossovers as bottom calls.** Napier's discipline is *convergence*.
- **Assuming central bank omnipotence.** SVB 2023 was a live example of policy failing to prevent stress. Don't over-anchor on "Fed will save us" (see `house_view/philosophy.md`).
- **Confusing bear-market bottom signals with routine cycle-position reads.** These signals apply specifically to deep drawdowns (>15% S&P), not to normal bull-cycle pullbacks.

## How to apply in reports

- **Weekly prep** — during any material drawdown, run the 3-signal dashboard. State clearly which signals are firing and which are absent.
- **Weekly look-back** — after a rally from a suspected bottom, attribute the recovery to which signals had fired first. Builds pattern calibration.
- **Daily wrap** — flag any material shift in the bond market (yield spike/blowout, spread widening/narrowing) as a potential regime signal for tomorrow's morning read.
- **Pitch tilt during suspected pre-bottom weeks** — when 2+ Napier signals fire, tilt weekly pitches toward income-quality names (dividend > bond yield candidates) with strong balance sheets.
