# Investor psychology pendulum

Marks' central metaphor: markets swing between excessive optimism and excessive pessimism, spending very little time at the rational midpoint. Psychology is real-time and observable, unlike fundamentals which are lagging. This is what makes psychology the *highest-signal* input for reading cycle position.

## The pendulum mechanism

- Prosperity → optimism rises → risk perceived as low
- Low perceived risk → willingness to accept less return per unit of risk
- More leverage, worse credit, weaker business models get funded → excess builds
- Trigger → psychology flips → risk perceived as everywhere
- Excess risk aversion → capital flees → forced selling → prices overshoot to the downside
- Wide spreads + trough valuations → seeds of the next swing

**The pendulum rarely stops at the midpoint.** Extremes are where the money is made (buying) or lost (chasing).

## The risk-attitude cycle — why "buy high, sell low" happens

Risk tolerance itself is cyclical and endogenous:

- Long calm + rising prices → confidence grows → recent (positive) experience becomes the template → risk feels benign
- Investors take more leverage, buy lower-quality names, extend duration, reduce covenant protection
- Losses eventually appear → psychology flips → risk feels omnipresent
- Same investors who saw no risk now see risk everywhere → they refuse to buy at the exact prices where risk/reward is best

This is why retail (and often institutional) capital chases performance. The bot's discipline must be **counter-cyclical to the crowd's risk appetite**.

## Concrete pendulum indicators — read where you are

### Peak (excessive optimism, defensive posture warranted)
- Credit spreads at historical lows
- IPO cadence high; quality declining; first-day pops routine
- Investors funding "story" companies with no near-term profitability path
- New issuance skewed to lowest-rated tiers
- Sentiment surveys at extreme optimism; bears rare in commentary
- P/E multiples materially above 10-year median
- Willingness to pay for narrative vs cash flow
- Refinancing is easy for all quality tiers
- Cov-lite dominates new HY issuance
- Media unanimity on bullish themes
- Old bears capitulating; new investors entering

### Trough (excessive pessimism, aggressive posture warranted)
- Credit spreads at historical highs
- IPO market frozen; even quality issuers can't price
- Lenders refuse regardless of borrower quality
- Only top-tier IG can access credit markets
- Sentiment at extreme pessimism; bulls rare
- P/E multiples at cycle lows
- Cash flow discipline dominates; stories dismissed
- Refinancing impossible for non-blue-chip
- HY defaults spiking
- Media fixated on worst-case scenarios
- Experienced investors have left; only forced sellers trade

## Marks' pendulum dashboard (checklist form)

| Dimension | Peak (defensive) | Trough (aggressive) |
|---|---|---|
| Economy | Overheating | Contracting |
| Outlook | Universally positive | Universally negative |
| Lenders | Eager, competing | Reticent, selective |
| Capital markets | Loose | Tight |
| Loan terms | Loose covenants | Restrictive covenants |
| Interest rates | Low | High (or rising fast) |
| Credit spreads | Narrow | Wide |
| Investor mood | Sanguine | Distressed |
| Asset prices | High | Low |
| Prospective returns | Low | High |
| Actual risk | High (disguised as low) | Low (disguised as high) |
| Dominant mistake | Buying too much | Buying too little |

Tick which column dominates the current environment. If most ticks are peak → defensive. Trough → aggressive. Mixed → mid-cycle discipline.

## Why psychology leads fundamentals

Fundamentals are lagging. By the time earnings decline is visible in reported numbers, credit has already tightened, forced sellers have already appeared, and the multiple has already compressed.

Psychology is real-time — credit spreads, IPO cadence, cov-lite share, sentiment surveys all move *now*, not next quarter. **A pitch generator that watches only trailing multiples will always be late.**

Example (2007-08): the crisis was psychological before it was fundamental. Lenders financed unsound mortgages for years on extrapolation ("home prices never fall"). When belief flipped, credit froze in weeks. The economic damage followed the psychological reversal by months.

## How to act at extremes

**At peaks:**
- Refuse to buy; consider trimming
- Accept that the market can stay irrational longer than you can stay short
- Watch for: "It's different this time," weak-business funding, cov-lite surge, media unanimity, absent skepticism
- Asymmetry: overpaying risk >> missing-upside risk. Be defensive.

**At troughs:**
- Deploy capital aggressively into quality
- Recognize that fear is excessive, not prescient
- Watch for: "Everything gets worse forever," refusal to lend regardless of quality, historic valuation lows, universal despair, forced-seller flow
- Asymmetry: underpaying risk is minimal; waiting-for-a-lower-bottom risk is real. Act.

## Common wrongness

- **Mistaking personal bias for market sentiment.** If you're bearish but spreads are tight and cov-lite dominates, the market is bullish. Your view isn't the market's view.
- **Confusing volatility with fear.** A 5% correction is noise. A 30% decline with frozen credit is a signal.
- **Missing regime shifts.** Dot-com, GFC, COVID each violated decades-old assumptions in weeks. When credit spreads blow past historical ranges, old rules are broken.
- **Recency bias in training.** A bot calibrated on 2010-2020 (mostly bull) will be structurally bullish. Weight crisis periods in the priors.
- **Being right for the wrong reason.** Buying at 5× earnings because "everything is cheap" and watching it fall to 3× is not success. Buy only when *both* valuation *and* pendulum position support the entry.

## How to apply in reports

- **Daily morning** — reference the current pendulum read in setting the tone of the pitch tilt (defensive names when psychology is euphoric; higher-torque names when psychology is fearful).
- **Weekly prep** — explicit statement of pendulum dashboard: which column dominates.
- **Weekly look-back** — attribute pitch outcomes to fundamentals vs psychology. When psychology dominated, was our read correct?
- **Daily wrap** — flag any single-day shift in psychology indicators (VIX spike, credit spread jump, forced-selling flow reports).
