# XOM — Facts

## Business
- Largest US integrated oil major by market cap; fully integrated upstream + downstream + chemicals.
- Revenue mix: Upstream (E&P) ~30-35% of revenue but ~65% of earnings depending on oil price; Downstream (refining/marketing) ~55-60% of revenue; Chemicals ~10%.
- Key upstream assets: Permian Basin, Guyana Stabroek block (co-op with Hess post-acquisition), LNG (PNG, Qatar).
- Gross margin cyclical (15-25%); operating margin swings 8-20% with crack spreads and Brent.
- FCF break-even in the $35-40/bbl Brent range post-2020 restructuring — significantly improved from pre-COVID $60+ break-even.
- Dividend aristocrat status (40+ consecutive years of increases); buyback program scaled up materially post-2022.

## Base rates
- Post-earnings implied move 3-4%; XOM reactions are typically dominated by the commodity tape between prints, not the print itself.
- Reports late-Jan/early-Feb, late-Apr/early-May, late-Jul/early-Aug, late-Oct/early-Nov.
- Guidance behavior: no explicit forward EPS guidance; capex + production ranges only. Reactions hinge on capex discipline commentary.
- Strongest seasonal window historically late-summer through winter (driving-season + heating demand overlap).
- Post-earnings drift is minimal — the stock resumes tracking Brent within 1-2 sessions.

## Key catalysts
- Earnings: late-Jan/early-Feb, late-Apr/early-May, late-Jul/early-Aug, late-Oct/early-Nov.
- OPEC+ meetings (typically monthly JMMC + semiannual full ministerial) — production quota decisions.
- Weekly EIA petroleum status report (Wednesday 10:30 ET) — crude/product inventories.
- Monthly IEA + OPEC + EIA STEO demand forecasts — sets the tape.
- Geopolitical: Middle East supply risk, Russia sanctions regime, Venezuela sanctions status.
- Guyana ramp milestones — the highest-return growth barrel in the portfolio.

## Correlations
- Beta to SPX ~0.9 but the loading is misleading — XOM is fundamentally a Brent/WTI proxy on multi-month horizons.
- 60-day correlation with Brent typically 0.60-0.85.
- Correlation with XLE 0.85-0.95.
- Modest positive correlation with DXY-weighted commodity basket; strong DXY typically pressures oil and XOM together.
- Inverse to 10Y real yields is weak; XOM is more inflation-hedge than duration play.

## Common wrongness
- "Peak oil demand is here" — recurring since 2019; global crude demand has continued to grow each year absent recession.
- "Energy transition kills the majors" — capex discipline + FCF has pushed shareholder returns to record levels through the transition.
- Overweighting single EIA prints — weekly inventory noise reverses often within 2 weeks.
- "Refining margins will normalize down permanently" — crack spreads are volatile but structurally supported by post-2020 refinery closures.
