# HD — Facts

## Business
- Largest US home improvement retailer; ~2,300+ stores across US, Canada, Mexico.
- Revenue mix by customer: Pro (contractors, tradespeople) ~50% and rising; DIY ~50% and declining as a mix share.
- Revenue mix by category: Building materials, decor, hardlines — roughly balanced; big-ticket discretionary (appliances, flooring, kitchens) is the interest-rate-sensitive line.
- Gross margin ~33%; operating margin ~14-15% — top-tier for retail.
- Growth drivers: Pro customer wallet share (SRS Distribution acquisition 2024 accelerates this), digital + interconnected retail, complex Pro job supply chain.
- Fiscal year ends late-January.

## Base rates
- Post-earnings implied move 4-6%; realized often larger when comps surprise materially.
- Reports late-Feb, mid-May, mid-Aug, mid-Nov — always paired with LOW read-through.
- Guidance behavior: explicit full-year comp + operating margin + EPS ranges; historically conservative.
- Strongest fiscal quarter Q2 (May-July) — spring project season.
- Post-earnings drift: positive Pro-mix commentary extends 5-10 trading days.

## Key catalysts
- Earnings: late-Feb, mid-May, mid-Aug, mid-Nov.
- Existing home sales (NAR, monthly, ~20th) — direct comp read; low turnover = weak HD demand.
- 30-year mortgage rate — housing turnover + big-ticket discretionary driver.
- Housing starts / permits (Census, monthly, mid-month) — Pro segment demand read.
- Case-Shiller home price data — home equity extraction feeds remodel demand.
- Weather events (hurricanes, major storms) — near-term positive catalyst on repair demand.
- LOW earnings — direct sector read-through, typically one day after HD.

## Correlations
- Beta to SPX ~1.0-1.1.
- Correlation with XHB (homebuilder ETF) 0.55-0.70.
- Inverse correlation with 30Y mortgage rate on multi-month horizons.
- Weak DXY sensitivity; ~10% ex-US revenue.
- Correlation with consumer discretionary basket (XLY) 0.65-0.80.

## Common wrongness
- "Housing rollover kills HD" — HD outperforms during turnover slowdowns when Pro/repair-remodel demand offsets DIY weakness.
- "High mortgage rates permanently break the model" — lock-in effect reduces turnover but increases repair-remodel demand as owners stay put.
- Underestimating Pro growth trajectory — SRS Distribution deal is a step-change, not a bolt-on.
- "DIY normalization is a permanent headwind" — DIY normalized in 2022-2023; Pro strength has more than offset.
