# MA — Facts

## Business
- Global payment network duopolist with Visa; open-loop network processing card transactions across issuers, acquirers, and merchants.
- Revenue mix: Payment network fees ~55% (domestic assessment + international transaction fees) — cross-border is the highest-margin line; Value-added services & solutions (data analytics, cybersecurity, consulting) ~30% and growing double-digit; Other ~15%.
- Gross margin ~100% (near-zero COGS — a pure network); operating margin ~55-58% — one of the highest in the S&P.
- Volume growth driver = global cash-to-electronic conversion + cross-border travel; cross-border is roughly 2x more profitable per dollar than domestic.
- FX pricing: revenues net of rebates/incentives; strong USD is a modest headwind to reported growth.

## Base rates
- Post-earnings implied move 3-5%; realized often smaller — MA is a low-vol quality compounder.
- Reports late-Jan, late-Apr, late-Jul, late-Oct — always one week after V; V print sets read-through.
- Guidance behavior: qualitative full-year framework, not explicit EPS. Reactions hinge on cross-border volume commentary.
- Strongest calendar quarters historically Q2 and Q3 as travel/summer volumes lift cross-border mix.
- Post-earnings drift: positive cross-border prints extend 3-7 trading days.

## Key catalysts
- Earnings: late-Jan, late-Apr, late-Jul, late-Oct.
- V earnings the week before — direct read-through and MA reaction preview.
- Monthly SpendingPulse data (proprietary MA release) — early consumer spending signal.
- Cross-border travel data (TSA, IATA) — cross-border volume proxy.
- DOJ/EU antitrust cases (interchange, merchant routing rules) — episodic negative catalysts.
- Fed rate cycle — MA is not a lender but consumer credit health affects volumes.

## Correlations
- Beta to SPX ~1.0-1.1; behaves more like a quality-growth compounder than a financial.
- Correlation with V 0.85-0.95 — near-identical tape most weeks.
- Weak correlation with XLF; do not treat as a bank.
- Inverse to DXY on multi-week horizons via cross-border FX exposure.
- Low correlation with 10Y yield.

## Common wrongness
- "Fintech disruption (Stripe, Adyen, Block) will erode the network" — recurring since 2015; MA volumes have continued to grow above nominal GDP + inflation.
- "Stablecoins/CBDCs will bypass the networks" — regulatory + merchant acceptance friction has kept this a decade-out risk, not near-term.
- Overweighting antitrust rulings — impacts have historically been narrow (interchange caps in specific geographies).
- Underestimating value-added services growth — the "software attach" on the network is a rising share of profit.
