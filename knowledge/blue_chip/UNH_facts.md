# UNH — Facts

## Business
- Largest US managed care organization by revenue and market cap; diversified across insurance (UnitedHealthcare) and health services (Optum).
- Revenue mix: UnitedHealthcare (insurance premiums) ~55-60%; Optum Health (care delivery) ~20%; Optum Rx (PBM) ~15%; Optum Insight (data/analytics) ~5%.
- Membership: ~50M+ commercial + Medicare Advantage + Medicaid + international; Medicare Advantage is the highest-growth line.
- Gross margin ~22%; operating margin ~8-9% — thin margins, but scale + Optum vertical integration drive durable EPS growth.
- Optum is the strategic differentiator vs peers (HUM, CVS, CI) — owned care delivery + PBM captures margin that pure insurers pay away.

## Base rates
- Post-earnings implied move 4-6%; can extend materially on Medical Loss Ratio (MLR) surprises.
- Guidance behavior: explicit full-year EPS range; MLR + membership guidance updated quarterly.
- Q1 is the most-scrutinized print due to Medicare Advantage annual reset — MLR trajectory sets the year.
- Reports mid-January, mid-April, mid-July, mid-October — first major managed-care print each cycle.
- Post-earnings drift is meaningful when MLR beats/misses — extends 5-10 trading days.

## Key catalysts
- Earnings: mid-Jan, mid-Apr, mid-Jul, mid-Oct.
- CMS Medicare Advantage rate notices — Advance Notice (early Feb) and Final Notice (early April). Direct EPS input.
- CMS Star Ratings release (October) — bonus payment eligibility.
- Election cycles — managed care valuation cycles pre/post-election on healthcare policy risk.
- COVID-era utilization normalization — MLR trajectory has been the key debate since 2023.
- DOJ antitrust scrutiny + Change Healthcare cyberattack aftermath — episodic overhangs.

## Correlations
- Beta to SPX ~0.7-0.8; low-beta on paper but idiosyncratic risk on MLR prints.
- Correlation with XLV 0.55-0.70; correlation with managed-care peers (HUM, CVS, CI) 0.70-0.85.
- Weak correlation with 10Y yield or DXY; UNH is a domestic policy-driven name.
- Inverse to healthcare policy risk (election proximity + Medicare-for-all rhetoric).

## Common wrongness
- "Medicare-for-all will end the managed care industry" — recurring every US election cycle since 2016; has not materialized and rarely will.
- "MLR normalization is a temporary Q1 issue" — the post-COVID utilization step-up has proven more structural than transitory.
- Underestimating Optum earnings contribution — the market often values UNH as an insurer when half the operating earnings come from vertically integrated services.
- "CMS rate cuts will crush MA margins" — cuts have been meaningful but UNH has consistently offset via mix, Stars, and pricing.
