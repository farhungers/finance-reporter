# V — Facts

## Business
- Payment network operator; toll-taker on card transaction volume — not a lender, no consumer credit exposure.
- Revenue mix (10-K): Service Revenues ~46-48%, Data Processing ~34-36%, International Transaction ~26-28%, Other ~5-7%; contra-revenue (client incentives) offsets ~28-30%.
- Cross-border volumes carry ~2-3x take-rate of domestic — international travel recovery is the single largest revenue-mix swing factor.
- Gross margin structurally ~80%+ (network economics); operating margin 65-67% — one of the highest in S&P 500.
- Two-sided network: 4B+ cards issued through ~14,500 financial institution partners; accepted at 130M+ merchant locations.
- Regulatory framework: designated systemically important financial market utility (SIFMU) in US; heavily regulated globally on interchange.

## Base rates
- Post-earnings implied move 3-5%; realized typically inside — low-vol among mega-caps.
- Payment volume growth (US + international) is the primary print watched; cross-border volume growth carries highest weight per point.
- Fiscal year ends Sept 30 — earnings cadence: late-Oct, late-Jan, late-Apr, late-Jul.
- Guidance behavior: explicit full-year net revenue growth + EPS growth ranges; typically raises through the year.
- IV crush post-earnings 40-50%.
- Seasonal: fiscal Q1 (holiday quarter, Oct-Dec) has highest absolute volumes; fiscal Q4 (Jul-Sep) is summer travel peak.

## Key catalysts
- Earnings: late-Oct, late-Jan, late-Apr, late-Jul.
- Cross-border travel prints (TSA throughput, monthly airline passenger data) — leading indicator.
- Interchange regulation: US Credit Card Competition Act (Durbin extension), EU/UK interchange caps, Reserve Bank of Australia — episodic negative catalyst.
- DOJ merchant lawsuit updates — long-tail; rarely acute mover.
- Consumer spending prints (retail sales, personal consumption) — background driver, not day-of.
- FX volatility — high FX vol boosts international transaction revenue via currency-conversion take.

## Correlations
- Beta to SPX ~0.95; beta to XLF ~0.85.
- Correlation with MA (Mastercard) 0.85-0.92 — highest single-name correlation in mega-cap complex; near-substitutes.
- Correlation with consumer discretionary basket 0.55-0.65 (spending exposure).
- Weak inverse to DXY on multi-week horizon via cross-border translation, but FX volatility itself is a tailwind.
- Correlation with financials sector muted — V is a network, not a bank; behaves like defensive growth compounder.

## Common wrongness
- "Stablecoins/CBDCs will disintermediate Visa" — recurring since 2018; adoption has not touched consumer point-of-sale share.
- "Interchange caps will crush margins" — recurring since Durbin (2011); V/MA have repeatedly re-priced or re-engineered around them.
- Overreacting to DOJ/regulatory headlines — historically 1-2 week drag then reversion.
- Underestimating cross-border recovery — post-COVID travel normalization has run longer than most models assumed.
- "Fintech will bypass the rails" — Klarna/Afterpay/Cash App still settle over Visa/MA rails; disintermediation narrative has consistently failed.
- See knowledge/sectors/financials.md — payment networks are their own sub-industry; do not conflate with bank NII sensitivity.
