# ORCL — Facts

## Business
- Enterprise software incumbent; database (Oracle Database, Exadata), applications SaaS (Fusion ERP, NetSuite), and cloud infrastructure (OCI).
- Revenue mix: Cloud services & license support ~75% (recurring, high-margin); Cloud license & on-premise license ~10%; Hardware ~7%; Services ~8%.
- OCI (Oracle Cloud Infrastructure) is the current growth engine — signed multi-year AI training capacity commitments including OpenAI (Stargate) that reshape the revenue trajectory.
- Gross margin ~75%; operating margin ~40-42% non-GAAP.
- RPO (Remaining Performance Obligations) — the disclosed multi-year contracted revenue backlog — is the key non-GAAP metric the Street tracks. Reached historic levels in FY24/FY25.
- Fiscal year ends late-May.

## Base rates
- Post-earnings implied move 6-8%; can extend materially when OCI or RPO guidance surprises.
- Reports mid-September, mid-December, mid-March, mid-June (Q1/Q2/Q3/Q4).
- Guidance behavior: explicit next-quarter revenue + EPS ranges; multi-year commentary on OCI capacity buildout.
- Historically underappreciated stock through 2019-2022; multiple has expanded materially as AI-cloud narrative took hold.
- Post-earnings drift: positive OCI prints extend 5-15 trading days; skepticism-cycle stock so beats compound.

## Key catalysts
- Earnings: mid-Sept, mid-Dec, mid-March, mid-June.
- Hyperscaler capex commentary (MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, META earnings) — signals AI infrastructure demand environment.
- NVDA earnings — GPU supply is the constraint for ORCL's OCI capacity expansion.
- Oracle CloudWorld conference (annual, September) — product + strategy showcase.
- Major OCI contract announcements (OpenAI/Stargate-scale) — episodic step-changes.
- Data-center construction milestones + capex pace commentary.

## Correlations
- Beta to SPX ~1.1-1.2.
- Correlation with mega-cap tech basket 0.55-0.75.
- Correlation with NVDA and AVGO has strengthened since 2024 as ORCL joined the AI-infra narrative.
- Modest inverse to 10Y yield via software duration.
- Weak DXY sensitivity; ~25% ex-US revenue.

## Common wrongness
- "Oracle is a slow-growth legacy database company" — the narrative that persisted through 2019-2022 that OCI would never catch AWS/Azure has broken.
- "AI capacity is bought, not earned" — OCI's ability to deliver dense GPU capacity at scale has become a competitive differentiator, not a commodity.
- Underweighting the recurring/license support base — provides EPS floor while OCI scales.
- "Larry Ellison risk" — succession + capital allocation questions recur but haven't materialized.
