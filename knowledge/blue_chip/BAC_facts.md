# BAC — Facts

## Business
- Second-largest US money-center bank by assets; universal bank with strong consumer + wealth + capital markets footprint.
- Revenue mix: Consumer Banking ~40% (deposits, credit card, mortgage); Global Wealth & Investment Management ~22% (Merrill, private bank); Global Banking ~22% (commercial banking, investment banking); Global Markets ~18% (trading, sales).
- Net interest income (NII) is roughly 55-60% of total revenue; heavily deposit-funded — low deposit-beta franchise is a structural advantage.
- Efficiency ratio typically 60-65%.
- Held-to-maturity (HTM) unrealized losses from 2020-2021 duration mismatch are the well-known balance-sheet overhang — rolls off with time + rate normalization.
- CET1 ratio typically 11-12%; regulatory buffer support drives capital return capacity.

## Base rates
- Post-earnings implied move 3-4%; realized often smaller — big-bank prints are usually tightly consensused.
- Reports mid-January, mid-April, mid-July, mid-October — after JPM (typically one day later); JPM print sets read-through.
- Guidance behavior: explicit NII quarterly guide + full-year expense range; reactions hinge on NII trajectory.
- Post-earnings drift: NII beats drive 3-7 day outperformance vs XLF.
- Strongest performance historically in rising-rate environments before deposit beta catches up.

## Key catalysts
- Earnings: mid-Jan, mid-Apr, mid-Jul, mid-Oct.
- JPM earnings the day before — direct read-through.
- Fed CCAR / stress test results (June) — determines buyback + dividend capacity.
- FOMC decisions + dot plot — direct NII input via forward rate expectations.
- Yield curve shape (2s10s) — flattening compresses NII outlook; steepening expands.
- Credit-quality data (delinquencies, charge-offs) — quarterly disclosures + monthly consumer credit data.

## Correlations
- Beta to SPX ~1.2-1.3.
- Correlation with XLF 0.85-0.95.
- Correlation with JPM 0.80-0.90.
- Positive correlation with 10Y yield on multi-week horizons; among the highest rate-sensitivity in mega-cap financials.
- Positive correlation with 2s10s steepness.

## Common wrongness
- "AOCI/HTM losses are a real capital problem" — recurring since 2023; the losses do not affect regulatory capital and roll off with maturity.
- "Deposit beta has caught up" — BAC's consumer deposit franchise has kept beta materially below peers.
- Underweighting Merrill wealth as a growth annuity — fee-based revenue is a rising share of profit.
- "Credit normalization = recession" — normalization from post-COVID lows is not equivalent to a credit cycle.
