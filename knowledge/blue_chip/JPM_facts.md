# JPM — Facts

## Business
- Largest US bank by assets; universal-bank model spanning consumer + commercial + investment banking + asset management.
- Revenue mix (10-K): Consumer & Community Banking (CCB) ~42-44%, Corporate & Investment Bank (CIB) ~35-38%, Asset & Wealth Management (AWM) ~12-14%, Commercial Banking ~8-10%.
- Net interest income (NII) is ~55-60% of revenue; non-interest income (fees, trading, IB) is the balance.
- CET1 ratio consistently 13-15% — well above regulatory minimum; buyback + dividend capacity is a function of stress-test outcomes and Basel III endgame rules.
- Return on tangible common equity (ROTCE) durably 17-22% — best-in-class for scale banks.
- Trading revenue split: Fixed Income Markets ~65-70% of markets revenue, Equity Markets ~30-35%; volatility regimes drive both.

## Base rates
- Post-earnings implied move 3-5%; realized typically clusters at low end.
- NII guide (full-year) is the single most-watched number; +/- $1B vs guide moves stock 2-4%.
- Trading revenue guidance is qualitative ("mid-single-digit growth"); actual print vs prior-year comp is the trade.
- Investment banking fee pool (M&A + ECM + DCM) is the sentiment lever for the CIB segment.
- Credit quality: net charge-offs (NCOs) and reserve build/release are the key stress indicators — Q4 typically has largest reserve moves.
- Guidance behavior: explicit full-year NII guide; qualitative color on credit + IB + expenses.

## Key catalysts
- Earnings: mid-Jan, mid-Apr, mid-Jul, mid-Oct — first mega-bank to report each quarter, sets the bank-earnings tape.
- Fed stress test results (late-June) — dictates capital return capacity for coming year.
- FOMC decisions — bank NII sensitivity to short-rate direction and yield-curve shape.
- Basel III endgame / capital rule announcements — episodic; can drive 3-5% sector moves.
- Investor Day (late-May) — multi-year targets refresh; low intraday impact but sets sell-side models.
- Credit cycle inflection signals (unemployment, credit card delinquencies, C&I loan demand) — leading indicators for reserve build.

## Correlations
- Beta to SPX ~1.1; beta to XLF (financials ETF) ~1.15.
- Correlation with money-center bank basket (BAC, C, WFC) 0.80-0.90 — highest intra-sector.
- Positive correlation with 10Y-2Y curve steepening — steeper curve improves NII outlook.
- Correlation with high-yield credit spreads inverse — spread widening pressures both credit book and capital markets fees.
- Correlation with VIX inverse in stable regimes; positive in trading-benefit regimes (elevated FICC when vol is high but orderly).

## Common wrongness
- "NII is peaking / rolling over" — narrative recurs every quarter deposits reprice; guide has repeatedly held.
- "Credit will crack" — recurring since 2022; NCOs have normalized but from very low base, not spiked.
- Overreacting to Dimon commentary — hedge language ("storm clouds", "later than expected") is stylistic; operational execution has been consistent.
- Underestimating fortress-balance-sheet premium — JPM trades at premium P/B (1.8-2.2x) vs peers (1.0-1.5x); the premium is durable.
- See knowledge/sectors/financials.md — money-center banks are curve-shape trades, not curve-level trades; watch 2s10s over absolute yield.
