# NFLX — Facts

## Business
- Largest global subscription streaming service; ~300M+ paid memberships across ~190 countries.
- Revenue mix: Streaming subscription ~95%; Ads-tier revenue (launched late-2022) ~5% and growing rapidly.
- Growth engines: paid sharing (password-sharing crackdown 2023) has been the biggest single revenue tailwind since the subscriber recession of 2022; ad-tier is the next multi-year lever.
- Gross margin ~46%; operating margin ~26-28% and expanding — scale + content-cost discipline drove margin re-rating.
- FCF turned positive in 2023 and now generates $6-8B+ annualized — end of the "content burn" era.
- No longer discloses paid membership numbers each quarter as of 2025 — Street focuses on revenue growth + margin + engagement.

## Base rates
- Post-earnings implied move 8-10%; realized often equals or exceeds — NFLX is one of the highest-volatility mega-caps around earnings.
- Reports mid-January, mid-April, mid-July, mid-October — first major streaming print each cycle.
- Guidance behavior: explicit next-quarter revenue + operating margin range; qualitative full-year framework.
- IV crush post-earnings 50-60% overnight — meaningful.
- Post-earnings drift: momentum on beats has been strong — extends 10-15 trading days.

## Key catalysts
- Earnings: mid-Jan, mid-Apr, mid-Jul, mid-Oct.
- Ad-tier scaling milestones + upfront commentary — key growth-lever debate.
- Major content release windows (Stranger Things final season, Squid Game seasons, live sports acquisitions).
- Live sports rights auctions (NFL Christmas games, WWE Raw partnership, boxing events) — narrative and engagement drivers.
- Price increase announcements — historically drives immediate re-rating in the ARM (average revenue per member) trajectory.
- Competitive streaming updates (DIS+, WBD Max, AMZN Prime Video, AAPL TV+).

## Correlations
- Beta to SPX ~1.3-1.5; historically high-beta.
- Correlation with mega-cap tech basket 0.55-0.70.
- Weak correlation with 10Y yield despite growth-stock reputation — cash-flow durability has broken the duration trade.
- Modest inverse to DXY via ~55% ex-US revenue.
- Low correlation with traditional media stocks (DIS, WBD, PARA) — NFLX now trades as a tech-quality name, not a media stock.

## Common wrongness
- "Streaming is a race to the bottom" — recurring since 2019; NFLX has demonstrated durable pricing power through multiple price increases.
- "Ad-tier will cannibalize higher-ARPU subscribers" — ad-tier has been mostly incremental to date.
- "Sports rights will destroy margins" — NFLX has taken a disciplined path (event-based, not full-season rights).
- Underweighting engagement/hours as the true competitive moat.
