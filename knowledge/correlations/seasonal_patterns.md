# Seasonal patterns

## Core rule
Most publicized seasonals are statistical noise or arbitraged away. A handful survive scrutiny — treat those as weak background bias, never as standalone signal. Seasonality is a tiebreaker, not a trade rationale.

## Base rates
- Sell-in-May: May-October US equity returns are lower than November-April in the historical record, but the gap has narrowed since 2010 and the effect is modest (~2-4% differential per year, not the "avoid summer" caricature).
- Santa Rally (last 5 sessions of December + first 2 of January): positive ~75% of years historically; small median gain (~1-2%).
- January effect (small-caps outperform in January): once robust, largely arbitraged since ETFs and index-fund flows scaled.
- September is historically the weakest S&P month — modestly negative average return; the effect is real but small.
- October has the highest realized volatility but average return is not the worst — the crash narrative overshadows the base rate.

## Sector-specific seasonals
- Energy: seasonal demand pattern for natural gas (winter heating, summer cooling) is real but weakened by shale-era supply flexibility.
- Retail / consumer discretionary: Q4 earnings-season strength around holidays; watch guidance more than the seasonal itself.
- Agricultural commodities: planting/harvest seasonals still meaningful for corn, wheat, soybeans.
- Precious metals: modest Q1 strength historically tied to Indian wedding-season and Chinese New Year demand.

## When it works
- Multi-year data supports the seasonal AND fundamentals don't contradict it — both need to align.
- Sector seasonals with real supply/demand drivers (energy, agriculture) more reliable than pure calendar effects.
- Seasonal as tiebreaker between two otherwise equally-rated pitches.
- Long horizons — seasonal edge is small per week, only accumulates over months.

## When it fails
- Any single year — seasonal odds are 60-70%, not 95%; a "seasonal is a lay-up" thesis is wrong ~30-40% of the time.
- Macro regime shifts override seasonality — 2022 saw a bad Santa Rally amid tightening fears.
- Well-known seasonals get faded by professionals — the more famous the seasonal, the more compressed the edge.
- News-driven markets — a real macro catalyst overwhelms every seasonal.

## Application
- Weekly prep: seasonal context belongs in "macro setup going in" as a 1-line background bias, not as the headline theme.
- Rubric use: seasonality alone does NOT earn `base_rate_support` — needs pairing with an additional historical pattern (post-event drift, structural cycle).
- Never write a pitch thesis whose primary rationale is a calendar seasonal.
- Watch for over-attribution — attributing normal market noise to a seasonal pattern is a common analysis error.
